## F60. Motion reveals a distinction that prepared rest conceals

Let the same unit-link, unit-mass horizontal arm have endpoint p(q), and let the two configurations at orientation zero be qA=(-phi/2,phi), qB=-qA, with 0<phi<pi. Match their actual Cartesian velocity (vr,vt), not their joint velocities. Write s=sin(phi/2), c=cos(phi/2), D=2-cos(phi)^2. Independent joint and polar kinetic-energy derivations give

~~~text
passive acceleration B minus A =
    [-2*s*vr*vt/D, (s*vt^2+c^2*vr^2/s)/D].
~~~

The tangential component is strictly positive for every nonzero matched velocity. A common joint command changes only the radial difference, so cannot cancel that complete instantaneous separation. At rest with zero input the histories remain identical indefinitely. The observation map (q,v)->(p,Jv) has determinant det(J)^2 locally; that local rank does not remove the two global branches, noise or singular endpoints.

The external finite-history instrument uses 54 matched pairs, 108 passive episodes and known candidate mean paths. At speed .03 m/s, oracle equal-prior Gaussian discrimination error is approximately .497763/.430374/.197066 after .1/.5/1 sec; at .1 m/s it is .475137/.054423/.0000178844. No sampled noisy learner is tested in those numbers. Locally mean-path separation is O(V^2 t^2), and its Gaussian information scales as O(V^4 t^4/sensor_variance) for a fixed sampling pattern. Longer waiting can help but costs time. No intrinsic nonzero speed threshold or mandatory active probe follows.

A counterexample prevents conflating configuration evidence with command knowledge: multiplying both masses by k leaves zero-input configuration histories unchanged, while torque response scales by 1/k. Passive identification of motion context is not identification of all action effects. `cartesian_moving_v17_theory_independent.md`, executable polar/Lagrangian checks and independent Hamiltonian/Radau replay preserve derivations, sign conventions, nonsingular-domain limits and numerical results.

## F61. A bounded post hoc action-risk detour, without reselection

Under randomized conditional commands U and arbitrary fixed causal baseline d(H), linear projection G(H)=E[Y U^T|H] Cov(U|H)^-1, with nonsingular conditional covariance, can expose the projected command effect when E[U|H]=0. This need not be an instantaneous derivative or a complete action response; nonlinear U^2 effects can have zero projection. Removing an arbitrary baseline preserves expectation but can increase variance.

For any supplied prediction m(H,U), an independent command V from the same conditional randomization law gives D=m(H,U)-m(H,V). The expectation of .5||D||^2-2(Y-d(H))^T D is the centered conditional-mean prediction risk, up to a predictor-independent constant. This elementary pair identity avoids a quadrature mechanism. It does not recover latent variation, guarantee conditional-law accuracy, remove policy shift or make a finite raw history sufficient. The relation to conventional orthogonalized outcome learning is acknowledged; no novelty is claimed.

The explicitly post hoc pilot scores all eight existing candidates on 960 ordinary validation rows, with eight shared virtual commands per row and two declared drift baselines. No model is reselected. These validation trajectories already informed the original selection, so selected-model scores are not independent confirmation. For the selected neural and local models, member scores with the common neural drift baseline are +1.56372e-7 +/- 5.88480e-7 and -1.76828e-6 +/- 1.10620e-6 descriptive trajectory SE. Alternative within-family differences are small relative to those SEs. This does not establish a loss-allocation explanation or justify a rescue learner.
