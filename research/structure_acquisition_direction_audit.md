**Recommendation: one bounded exploratory screen is defensible.** The question
is whether physical experience can acquire computations that improve later
short-support learning beyond competent continued neural learning. This changes
the available feature family, unlike the two retired formulations. Those failures
do not, however, establish that fixed features were their actual bottleneck.

The operation is scoped symbolic meta-feature construction followed by ordinary
Gaussian coefficient learning and conditioning. Neural models also acquire
computations through parameter updates; no expressivity impossibility applies to
them. An advantage would concern useful inductive bias, experience efficiency or
computation under the tested budgets. Feature counts alone measure none of these.

## Closest established accounts

I read the actual method sections of
[Alet et al., Modular meta-learning (CoRL 2018)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v87/alet18a/alet18a.pdf).
Equation 2 learns module parameters using held-out task error after selecting a
structure on task training data. Equation 4 incorporates parameter adaptation
into that objective. Its permitted composition schemes include trees whose
modifications add or delete nodes. BOUNCEGRAD combines simulated-annealing
structure search and gradient updates. Thus reusable compositions and
support-to-query selection are established ideas. Growing program features and
analytic coefficient adaptation are a particular construction, not a new general
learning principle.

I also read Section 3 and Algorithm 1 of
[Sen Fong and Motani, Multi-Level Symbolic Regression (AISTATS 2024)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v238/sen-fong24a/sen-fong24a.pdf).
MSR fits group-specific numerical parameters for each common candidate function
structure using parallel DistilSR processes, then selects a structure by the
harmonic mean of fitted group MSEs. Its stated score differs from short-support
adaptation followed by disjoint-query evaluation. The matched fit-grown DAG is
therefore necessary to attribute an advantage to this selection difference; the
frozen old DAG alone only tests expansion. DreamCoder/library-learning precedent
also prevents treating executable reuse itself as novel.

## Decisive interpretation and falsifier

The main evidence must come from later fresh bodies after acquisition. The
continued neural ALPaCA and whole-network CoDA competitors receive the same new
experience and replay, so the established account has a serious opportunity to
explain the result. Frozen-old and fit-grown DAG controls distinguish expansion
from the selection criterion. Repeatedly searched query episodes are training
data, not independent confirmation. The frozen gate should decide continuation;
better acquisition-data fit or readable expressions cannot rescue a failure.

Cubic actuation and quadratic drag align especially well with multiplication and
`abs`. The grammar is substantial supplied knowledge, even though compound terms
and hidden coefficients are withheld. A win is conditional on this family and
grammar; it would earn a different-family test, not demonstrate universal
structure discovery. The finite-time velocity-change target need not possess
the simulator's compact instantaneous equation.

Search, normalization, deduplication and prior fitting can all matter. Probe-based
semantic pruning is numerical training-support pruning, not global equivalence.
Report actual search termination and total pretraining/tuning costs. The failed
eight-substep spot check and prospective sixteen-substep correction are an
instrument repair, not evidence for the learning hypothesis; preserve both.

No diagnostic expansion, control experiment or new grammar is recommended before
the first frozen comparison. Failure should retire this formulation. Success
would remain exploratory pending replication and the stronger checks already
specified in the plan.

There is a useful narrow construction result: both grown pipelines improve these
new-law means over continuing only the old DAG's coefficient prior, and correct
support helps the meta-grown learner relative to wrong-body support. Experience
and expansion are doing useful work in this screen. This is not an overall win
over continued neural learning, nor a clean attribution to one selected fragment
or its mathematical syntax.

Meta-grown features fail the main new-law ID criterion at both budgets: CoDA is
better at 16 support, and neural ALPaCA is better at 64. Meta-grown retention is
also worse than both neural competitors at both budgets. The OOD ranking is
mixed: neural ALPaCA beats meta at 16, while meta is best among these five methods
at 64. CoDA's poor OOD result must remain visible, but selecting that comparison
would not satisfy the frozen question. No single neural competitor dominates
every condition, and none needs to for this formulation's gate to fail.

The proposed selection criterion has not earned its continuation claim either.
Meta improves new-law ID mean over fit-grown by about 7.1% at 16 support, below
the prespecified 10%; at 64 support, fit-grown is better. Fit-grown also wins OOD
at 16 and retention at both budgets. These observations reject the claimed
practical advantage at this budget; they do not establish a general theorem
against support-to-query structure selection.

This remains one trained seed and one acquisition corpus. The result does not
locate the failure uniquely in search, grammar, prior learning or capacity, and
does not demonstrate catastrophic forgetting without a before/after comparison.
Those unresolved explanations do not justify a rescue: neither the frozen gate
nor the present scientific evidence calls for new fits, altered grammar, a larger
benchmark or control experiments. Close this feature-acquisition campaign and
preserve the useful expansion result together with the competitive failure. The
ultimate objective remains unresolved; this result selects no replacement
architecture or automatic fourth candidate.
