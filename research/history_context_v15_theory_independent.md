## Aliasing, exactly and under approximation

Let the input summary be only supplied current q and goal q, with an equal prior on initial velocities +v and -v. A controller receiving only that summary must have the same action distribution in both cases. A fixed learned-model index or independent random coin does not reveal the velocity sign. Merely fitting the pose-conditioned rest relation more accurately cannot resolve this missing distinction.

For the explicitly simplified ballistic/no-Coriolis surrogate, B=M(q)^-1 and

    qplus(T)-q=Tv+.5*T^2*B*u,
    qminus(T)-q=-Tv+.5*T^2*B*u.

Consequently the paired mean squared error is exactly

    T^2*||v||^2+.25*T^4*||B*u||^2.

It is minimized by u=0 for this surrogate, and randomizing independently of sign adds the nonnegative expected action term. When bounded commands uplus/minus=minus/plus(2/T)*M*v are feasible, sign-aware decisions cancel the surrogate endpoint error. This establishes a concrete information distinction without assuming that a variable named velocity must be the internal representation.

The physical plant is NOT this surrogate at nonzero velocity. It has

    a(q,v,u)=M(q)^-1*(u-h(q,v)),
    h(q,v)=sin(q2)*[-(2*v1*v2+v2^2),v1^2].

h is even in v, so opposite initial velocities have the same initial acceleration, including its Coriolis term. The original draft's bare ballistic display must not be presented as the physical Taylor expansion through orderT^2. This was reported independently and corrected before any milestone claim; source metadata now calls T^2||v||^2 a ballistic-surrogate reference.

For exact physical endpoints fplus(u),fminus(u), define midpoint m=(fplus+fminus)/2 and half-separation d=(fplus-fminus)/2. The elementary exact identity is

    .5*(||fplus-q||^2+||fminus-q||^2)
       =||m-q||^2+||d||^2.

Since qacc is even in v and u is constant, time reversal gives q(T;q,-v,u)=q(-T;q,v,u). Smooth expansion therefore yields

    m-q=.5*T^2*M^-1*(u-h)+O(T^4),
    d=T*v+O(T^3),
    paired physical risk=T^2*||v||^2+O(T^4).

These remainders are uniform for bounded actions and initial conditions on a suitable compact smooth-flow neighborhood. In particular nonzero v implies separation for sufficiently small T, so a common pose-only action cannot make both endpoints exactly q there. This does not supply a numerical finite-T lower bound without controlling the remainder uniformly. Zero torque, a sampled action grid or a local optimizer is not a global nonlinear pose-only optimum certificate at T=.2. Individual feasible commands likewise do not prove a global lower bound for the aliased competitor.

## Causal information and physical preparation

Four noisy past positions at times(-.2,-.15,-.1,-.05), exact current q/goal, known sample times, learned broad-data v14 corrected inverse relation and the action bound are ordinary controller inputs. Neither true current v, exact past velocities, oracle actions nor future positions/noise enter ordinary commands. Since the goal equals q, it itself supplies current pose; hiding q in another field would not remove this information.

Past trajectories are generated by solving the true zero-torque dynamics backward from the evaluator-chosen current condition, then checking forward return. Negative-time integration is an evaluator preparation device producing physically legitimate histories; it is not the ordinary controller querying the future. Such exact preparation is a strong experimenter capability, and the pilot does not learn to collect these paired histories in a continuous stream.

The anchored least-squares slope is supplied arithmetic:

    vhat=sum_i t_i*(oi-q)/sum_i t_i^2.

It is a causal history statistic, not a discovered representation. Conditional on the true past path, sensor-noise covariance is SD^2/sum_i(t_i^2)*I=1.3333333e-7*I (per-coordinate SD .000365148). Deterministic curvature contributes bias: Taylor expansion gives vhat=v+.5*a0*sum(t_i^3)/sum(t_i^2)+higher terms. Here sum(t_i^2)=.075 and .5*sum(t_i^3)/sum(t_i^2)=-1/12 seconds. Zero past torque does not make acceleration identically zero because h generally is nonzero.

The controller converts the supplied estimate to desired acceleration -2*vhat/T, then solves bounded displacement/acceleration relation least squares with learned v14 K (through its pseudoinverse B). The v14 relation was fitted to finite rest pulses at duration .1, whereas this use is nonrest at duration .2. Therefore this is a deliberately approximate transfer of learned local inertial response, together with a scientist-supplied compensating history operator. Good results would not mean that moving-system dynamics or the needed history length were learned from continuous experience.

## Direction challenge

The useful uncertainty is whether a learner can detect that a reusable summary loses distinctions needed for future decisions, then revise its use of experience. This pilot establishes a clean instrument and a working supplied-history construction. Another aliasing theorem, extra slope filter or expanded rest calibration would leave that uncertainty open.

A consequential follow-up must use continuous coupled experience to select or revise retained history and evaluate new decisions, with strong applicable history-based/observer alternatives sharing the information budget. Neither named velocity nor a particular state architecture is logically mandatory. The key claim should concern acquired distinctions and decision consequences, while explicitly separating the scientist's supplied coordinates, preparation, family and loss from what experience changes.
