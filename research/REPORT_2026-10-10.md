## Governing objective

Find a mechanism of learning with credible potential to change capabilities,
scientific understanding, or informed method choice. Reuse established
methods freely. Do not confuse a new name with a new mechanism or
synthetic benchmark success with consequential progress.

This work extends the initial handoff and Cycle 01. The leading hypothesis
was that learning how and where to selectively revise mechanisms might
yield better adaptation. Three other directions remain open:
causal abstraction discovery, predictive-state learning, and the
representation-versus-inference trade-off.

## Cycle 01: Known-structure diagnosis

Scripts: cycle01_sparse_repair.py and cycle01_validate.py.
Providing perfect prechange structure made selective Bayesian repair
efficient under matching assumptions but brittle otherwise.
A standard Bayesian mixture repaired the apparent failure when given
a broader change family. No novelty claim survived this control.

## Cycle 02: Unknown rule identities and adverse regimes

Code: cycle02_unknown_structure.py, cycle02_neural_cv.py,
cycle02_regression.py, cycle02_logistic.py.
Inputs: 10 binary variables, 6 binary outputs, label-flip noise 0.05.
Prechange: 96 observations. Postchange: 48 observations.
Exactly 2 output mechanisms change, locations hidden, onset known.
The Bayesian class is 1,335 conjunction/parity rules depending on up
to 3 inputs, but exact rules are not supplied.

Three regimes were tested: in-class, dense threshold, and a shift
from in-class rules to four-variable parity.
Each regime has 24 fixed seeds for initial baseline comparisons.

Mean final excess log loss, nats per output, after 48 new observations:

                              in-class    dense      novel parity4
Bayesian active grammar         ~0.000    0.70577       0.38105
Linear logistic with replay      0.33883  0.23682       0.40064
Modular neural with replay/CV    0.33444  0.25020       0.41227

The neural pretraining epoch was selected using 24 held-out PRECHANGE
observations, not evaluation labels, and the model was then refit to
all 96 initial observations. This is a modest diagnostic MLP, not a
state-of-the-art neural learning baseline. All neural and regression
models used random queries; Bayes active had a query-policy advantage.
The comparison therefore shows sensitivity to inductive assumptions
but does not isolate inference superiority or prove neural domination.

## Cycle 03: A tight mathematical representation barrier

Script: cycle03_parity_bound.py. Data: cycle03_parity_bound.json.
Under uniform input distribution, any prediction in the span of
Boolean functions depending on at most three inputs has zero
correlation with four-bit parity chi4. Therefore conditional
average predictions in the two parity classes are equal.
By conditional Jensen, the expected cross-entropy on noisy parity
is at least ln(2). The irreducible entropy is h(0.05), so

  L_excess >= ln(2) - h(0.05) = 0.494631937 nats.

This bound is tight since the constant prediction 0.5 attains it.
It is an instance of established Fourier orthogonality theory,
not a new general mathematical theorem. Increasing interactions,
observations, or optimization cannot solve this representational
obstruction without enlarging the hypothesis family.

Adding all 210 four-way parity rules to the original 1,335 options
permits near-zero excess predictive loss on the parity4 shifts
in these small environments. This is a prespecified richer model,
not a self-invented representation.

## Cycle 04: Evidence-certified local model expansion

Code: cycle04_certified_expansion.py, cycle04_replication.py.
The method tests whether postchange observations for an output
are consistent with ANY fixed low-degree rule, using a mixture
of parity-4 alternatives divided by the maximum likelihood under
the low-degree null. Let Q be the alt mixture and H0 null class:

  E_t = Q(Y[1:t] | X[1:t]) / max_(h in H0) P_h(Y[1:t] | X[1:t]).

For any true fixed h*, E_t <= Q/P_h*, a likelihood-ratio
martingale under h*. Ville's inequality therefore guarantees
P(ever E_t >= 1/alpha_j) <= alpha_j under specified assumptions.
Six simultaneous output checks use alpha_j = 0.05/6, threshold 120.
The guarantee remains valid for predictable adaptive input queries
but requires fixed postchange rules and known independent noise.
The detector uses existing universal-inference statistics.

Independent replication: 240 NEW seeds for EACH environment regime.
Mean final excess log loss (nats per output):

                          in-class   parity4       dense
Restricted Bayesian        0.004758   0.344345      0.682554
Always-expanded Bayes      0.004752   0.000159      0.686713
Certificate-expanded Bayes 0.004758   0.001959      0.685946

Under parity4, the certificate detected 478 of 480 genuinely
changed outputs; mean detection delay 23.18 interventions, median
22, 90th percentile 30. No false triggers occurred in the 240
within-class trials. Zero observed is NOT a zero-risk theorem.
In dense-threshold tests, the detector alerted for 6/480 changed
outputs, and 19/240 trials had an alert on an unchanged output.
The null of that detector is itself false in the dense regime.

Certified minus always-expanded final parity4 mean excess loss:
+0.001800, standard error 0.001264 across independent trials.
No superior predictive performance is established.

## Computational-cost audit

Per observation and six outputs, always-expanded Bayes evaluates
6*(1335+210)=9270 candidate likelihood factors.
Before any certificate triggers, the tested implementation uses
6*1335 factors for low-class updating plus 6*(1335+210) for
its composite-null/alternative diagnostic: 17280 factors,
or 1.864 times always-expanded. With two expanded outputs,
this grows to 20370, or 2.197 times always-expanded.
This excludes replay overhead, memory allocations, and normalization.
The current certificate is NOT computationally preferable to
simply representing the known fourth-order family from the outset.

## Interpretation, not claims of discovery

We established a precise impossibility under fixed representation,
and a reliable, anytime-valid mechanism for choosing when to expand
specific output hypothesis spaces if a candidate alternate family
has been provided. These are valid, bounded research results.

We did NOT discover the missing class automatically. A simple
always-expanded Bayesian method is stronger and cheaper in this toy
setting. The formal ingredients have concrete predecessors.

The high-upside open problem is to discover WHICH representational
operators to invent from raw observations, with a compelling cost
relative to flexible Bayes, program induction, causal representation
learning, and strong neural baselines. It is possible to have an
excellent structure-inadequacy detector and an uncompetitive learner.

## Research reorientation

Do not keep multiplying finite Boolean rule types and calling them
a new learning paradigm. Instead, prioritize hypotheses on how
learners can generate useful, reusable abstractions themselves,
and test against DreamCoder, CEGIS/abstraction refinement, and
Bayesian nonparametric alternatives. Retain predictive-state learning
as a substantially different candidate direction.

Next tests require hidden mechanisms, unknown shift times, matched
interaction/compute budgets, and adversarial conditions outside any
preloaded grammar. An alleged new learning principle must beat
simply expanding a standard Bayesian model under decision-relevant
constraints before a stronger claim is justified.
