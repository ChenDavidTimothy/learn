## Competing explanations remain live

**History and relevance metric.** A 16 delay interface and standardized nearest-context distance are supplied assumptions. They can merge histories with different action consequences or emphasize past commands that are poor similarity cues. Conversely old information can also enter through the retained library and shifted plan: this is not a strictly memoryless controller, so failure cannot prove any universal finite-history impossibility. Natural motion may contain useful information that this particular regression/metric fails to exploit.

**Policy/support shift.** Exogenous conservative acquisition and goal-directed slowing/stopping need not visit the same joint history/action neighborhoods. Scalar command ranges and interior initial positions do not establish support. Appending can improve proximity to a visited context without learning a globally correct physical law. The actual nearest-distance medians are lower for retained than frozen in all eight corpora, but that is not proof of adequate physical support or the causal explanation of improved control; recent self-generated rows are naturally close in this supplied metric. A bad first action can also create recovery conditions absent from either nominal witness or acquisition.

**Approximation and validation.** Six-block observed-position MSE on exogenous trajectories may select a relation with the wrong intervention gain or behavior near stopping. The pilot's weak command contrasts remain relevant negative evidence. An illustrative fresh mismatch is replicate 207: selected exogenous six-block test MSE about .0432 (the largest of these eight), yet retained task cost about .0875 (the smallest). Replicate 206 has lower test MSE about .0224 but retained cost about 42.87. These are descriptive counterexamples to treating that aggregate forecast score as a reliable ranking of this control behavior, not a post hoc selection rule or proof that prediction is useless.

**Uncertainty and inference.** Three episode-bootstrap mean paths do not represent every hidden circumstance, approximation error or unsupported context. Overconfident averaged consequences could cause poor actions. That hypothesis requires checking residuals/decision errors against the actual dispersion, rather than declaring that a new belief or latent-state architecture is necessary. Robust feedback or a better ordinary local estimate might be enough.

## Most consequential next question

**When does population experience help a new encounter more than estimating the currently useful local relationship from that encounter's own stream, and what observable evidence justifies that reuse?**

A retained prefix/local estimator is a serious rival because the existing warmup already supplies 17 noisy camera readings and four randomized held-pair commands, in addition to zero-input motion. The present h16 training-row rule cannot turn earlier prefix transitions into completed h16 rows; another conventional estimator could use those same observations more efficiently. It might need far less population data. However, a compact second-order or approximately constant local response assumption is additional supplied structure. If it succeeds, credit the simplicity/data efficiency and disclose that tradeoff; do not call it assumption-free acquisition of general dynamics. Do not give it extra calibration, exact initial rest/velocity or privileged state.

The prefix route is not prescribed as best. It could fail because 1.6 seconds is weakly informative at low speed, input directions are poorly conditioned, local response varies, or its structural approximation is wrong. A stronger generic history relation, episodic completion, a different relevance criterion or uncertainty-aware behavior are competing explanations. Choose among them with a small discriminating investigation rather than implementing all architectures.

Before new construction, separate two existing-data questions: do selected models predict the actual next camera readings under the actions they really issued, and do they predict how alternative available actions change those consequences at the same ordinary prefix? Teacher-forced on-policy errors address the first; evaluator-only fixed action forks at prespecified saved contexts address the second. Unlike additional learner probes, those diagnostic forks cannot update ordinary libraries or select settings. Contrast errors, model dispersion, nearby acquired histories and stopping/recovery conditions can then distinguish plausible support/approximation explanations. Accurate drift with wrong contrasts would directly challenge the learned action relation; accurate local consequences with bad task choices would implicate decision/uncertainty handling instead.

A subsequent comparison should freeze a justified lower-data local/prefix competitor and the current mechanism under equal ordinary time, sensing and command opportunities, then retain paired fresh contexts. Population experience should earn its acquisition cost rather than automatically be treated as useful knowledge. If the simple local estimate suffices, accept the established explanation. If population reuse materially helps only in declared contexts, identify those conditions. If neither works despite informative feasible conditions, that failure can justify learning a different relation. No stronger disturbance, larger architecture or new representation follows automatically from 55/192.

The general implication is limited but consequential: retaining more experience can improve decisions without making the acquired relation trustworthy across contexts. The next work should identify what makes reuse valid and useful, not optimize the number of stored rows or defend the pilot's apparent success.
