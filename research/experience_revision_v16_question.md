# Next framing: when should experience change a decision mechanism?

Unresolved, not a result. Do not begin by adding more lags or a new optimizer.
The continuous-history study supplies a fixed menu and broad independent experience.
Even success there would not establish that a learner can recognize when an old
relation no longer supports its ongoing decisions, acquire useful new evidence,
and recover within a limited interaction budget. Nor would successful adaptation
by itself establish intelligent generality or a need for a new learning paradigm.

First-principles alternatives:

- A robust controller may already tolerate the change; no further identification
  is necessary for that task. This conventional sufficiency is a serious outcome.
- Ordinary continual fitting on task-induced experience may suffice. Active probes
  and explicit change detectors must earn their cost against that baseline.
- The ongoing task may not excite a newly relevant action distinction. Then
  waiting for prediction residuals or collecting more nearly identical observations
  may not resolve the decision uncertainty; a targeted intervention might matter.
- Changing the task/summary, rather than learning a complete new body, may be the
  simplest repair. Prediction accuracy alone is not the objective.

Choose a minimal continuous change experiment only after v15 is audited. Keep
ordinary positions/actions, no state resets or hidden change-time/changed-parameter
announcement to any adaptive controller. Same total real interaction/torque/sensor
budget, counterfactual action support and fair robust/online conventional baselines.
Truth/change labels may score and diagnose but must not trigger learner updates.
Compare frozen knowledge, ordinary ongoing updates and a genuinely distinguishable
resource-allocation rule. Do not add active probing if ordinary updating is enough.
Keep detection delay, adaptation, task precision and collateral control cost separate.

A changed payload/inertia is physically relevant and robust PD may solve it already.
A changed actuator mapping exposes different information requirements but must have
a defensible physical/task motivation; do not engineer an arbitrary sign flip solely
to defeat a baseline. Check feasibility and derive what observation can distinguish
the alternatives BEFORE running. A finite post-change failure may be insufficient
support, estimation error, loss of control authority or an unreachable deadline;
do not assert that the learner simply needs a better representation.

Investigate relevant established adaptive control, dual control and online model
learning seriously after independently specifying the information/decision problem.
No specific architecture, theorem, environment change or active-probing method is
mandated here. The fixed objective is general learning from experience; the route
remains open. A new release requires consequential verified evidence, not this note.
